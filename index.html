The Demise of the Mother [Zeit Künstliche Besamung, Reproduktion]

An ecosystem abruptly erupts within the space, operating to expose the institutional violence embedded in the societal mandate of maternity and revealing the raw contingency and randomness of existence.

Mutated into the morphology of a virus, the body operates as an invasive prosthetic—expropriating reproduction from patriarchal and taxonomic governance to defiantly name a new species and mode of propagation.

Channeled through somatic labor, the duration of parting earth with bare hands to sow the ovum gestates an anonymous, non-human embryo within the computational void. Transcending biological division, this propagation operates as a viral contagion within the spectator's memory, stripping birth of its romanticized sanctity and transmuting it into a metabolic transaction between synthetic organs and raw code.

HISCOX Kunstpreis 2023 exhibition view

egg with wild flower seed

Extending beyond the gallery, the work operates as an apparatus drawing bodies into action. Participants receive biodegradable eggs containing dormant seeds, casting or depositing them wherever they choose. Free from prescribed routes, this arbitrary dispersal operates as a contingent gesture of existence—unfolding through unscripted material persistence outside systemic control.

How can pre-linguistic resonance help dissolve our closed selves and create a shared 'Public Skin'?

Public Skin

Today, our lives and bodies are continuously measured and categorized through names, data, and the logic of efficiency. In a world where everything is reduced to information and signs, authentic sensory contact fades away, leaving us face-to-face with the "end of experience," trapped within our closed selves.

What dismantles the root of this disconnection is a field of resonance operating prior to critical judgment. Before fully articulated language arrives, my work invokes a process of de-othering, summoning back into the realm of sensation those entities long exiled beyond systems of classification—nature and the female body. This connects directly to the mechanism of ancient festivals, which temporarily suspended social order and judgment to bring forth the banished and dissolve rigid boundaries.

Within the layered silence of darkness, the gathering of information and cognitive calculation halt; the viewer moves beyond being a mere onlooker to become a "somatic accomplice" (Somatische Komplizenschaft), physically absorbing tremors that lie beyond language. In this moment, as explanations and social labels fall away, the walls encircling the self dissolve. Through pre-linguistic resonance, viewers break free from their isolated worlds and finally acquire a "Public Skin" that directly connects them with once-alienated existences.

Dialogue begins where naming ceases: existence not as fixed matter, but as the chaotic potentiality of randomness effaced by systems. Form of absence. High-pressure air displaces wet paint without preliminary lines, cutting trajectories of void. Born from the friction of pressure, velocity, and the body, the human figure crystallizes only in the aftermath.

The Breathing void: Upon the contingent trajectories of non-being, colors are born, live their own lives, and fade.

Because we were never a finished entity—always a fleeting condensation of countless gestures—seek not the finalized image, but the threshold of its emergence. Art disrupts the one-sided order of naming: as in a communal rite, the fear of the estranged body is not explained away, but collectively endured. Where the name falls silent, the material surface transforms into a Public Skin: a field of resonance where bodies meet one another anew. Art not as a distant assertion, but as physical, immediate touch.

Painting becomes a physical act of sensing. My body registers pressure, velocity, and temperature in real time, allowing the figure to emerge from movement rather than predetermined form.

The core of this work lies not in defining form with hand or brush, but in a physical process that deconstructs the order of pigment through the immaterial force of high-pressure air. Air pressure pushes and displaces paint across the canvas surface, generating subtle layers of density.

Here, the shape of existence emerges not as a solid, filled mass of pigment, but within the space swept away by air—in the trajectories left behind by emptiness and void. Rather than static "Being," it is this 'trajectory of non-being' (Form des Nichtseins) that becomes the most vital point of origin for existence.

When unmoored from materiality, how can floating bodies recognize and reveal the 'Skin of Knowledge' as their boundaries fade?

The exhibition opens by constructing a dark space where visual signs are rendered powerless. Here, darkness functions not merely as a device to obscure objects from sight, but as a site of dissolution that unmoors the "anchor of materiality"—the force that once bound existence to rigid social positions and form.

In a space deprived of light, the bodily contours and solid boundaries that once demarcated the self from the other gradually blur and dissolve. Losing one's fixed coordinates and floating through the space is precisely the essential condition required to suspend critical judgment and conventional socialization, opening the way to resonance.

Skin of Knowledge

The Skin of Knowledge (Haut des Wissens) is an exploratory concept that designates the semiotic boundaries of contemporary society, where all existence is endlessly measured and categorized through data, algorithms, and the logic of efficiency. Overlaid by institutional language and fixed identities, this surface confines individual presence within normativity, frequently suspending the possibility of genuine sensory contact. Through its thick materiality, painting renders this hitherto invisible Skin of Knowledge perceptible, probing whether these rigid boundaries can be dissolved and transformed into a 'Public Skin' where existences beyond language can breathe together.

The material density of the acrylic paint and the weight of the canvas make the Skin of Knowledge tangibly felt. Thick, layered pigment and theatrical light mark the compressed surface of categorization—revealing the living existence that persists beneath it.

Through my body as a primordial sensor, I register woman, nature, and the invisible existences relegated to "others" by normative systems. Drawing the spectator into the somatic conditions of this exiled existence, I stage a direct confrontation—a ritual of de-othering.

Public Skin is a collective realm where sensation emerges prior to naming.

As distance grows from the material world, the layers of canvas and pigment thin out. The body loses its weight. What remains in the end is emotion, and a blind faith in materiality.

Artist Statement

We perceive the world only as far as the reach of our tether permits. The artist conceptualizes the systematized knowledge layered over existence—masquerading as skin—as the "skin of knowledge," summoning onto the canvas the inherent possibilities of being hidden beneath it. Rooted in the insight that language and capital can never fully exhaust existence, this work is a critical endeavor that introduces delicate fractures into our automated everyday perception.

In this process, painting ceases to be a mere tool of representation and becomes a physical act of forming a "public skin." Pushing color with an airbrush loaded with air rather than pigment generates random trajectories where bodily breath, velocity, and atmospheric chance intersect beyond deliberate intention. The human figures emerging on the canvas are not predetermined destinations, but the temporary condensation of countless non-linear events—acting as a visceral "roar of existence."

Thus, medium is redefined not as a tool of execution, but as a physical "substrate" through which sensation and existence filter. Spanning painting, sculpture, and digital environments, the practice utilizes the material resistance of each medium to recover a sensation prior to language. Rather than dogmatically proposing a new world, the artist defamiliarizes our prescribed roles and names, establishing an aesthetic device for dialogue that opens existence not to a singular conclusion, but to "chaotic possibilities."
