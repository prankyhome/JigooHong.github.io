The Demise of the Mother [Zeit Künstliche Besamung, Reproduktion]

Participants receive biodegradable eggs containing dormant wild flower seeds and carry them out into the streets, placing or casting them wherever they choose. Free from institutional control, this arbitrary dispersal becomes a collective gesture of life taking root in the public fabric of the city.

Inside the gallery, reproduction is stripped of its romanticized, patriarchal mandate. Through somatic labor—parting earth with bare hands to sow seeds—the work exposes the institutional violence that regulates bodies and maternal duty.

Extending into a virtual environment, life is no longer treated as an automated social obligation, but as an unpredictable, shared force that belongs directly to the hands that scatter it.

HISCOX Kunstpreis 2023 exhibition view

egg with wild flower seed

The work operates as an apparatus that pulls bodies into action. Rather than observing an artwork from a safe distance, visitors step into the ecosystem, taking physical responsibility for how and where these dormant seeds will awaken.

How can pre-linguistic resonance help dissolve our closed selves and create a shared 'Public Skin'?

Public Skin

Today, our lives and bodies are continuously measured and categorized through names, data, and the logic of efficiency. In a world where everything is reduced to information and signs, authentic sensory contact fades away, leaving us face-to-face with the "end of experience," trapped within our closed selves.

What dismantles the root of this disconnection is a field of resonance operating prior to critical judgment. Before fully articulated language arrives, my work invokes a process of de-othering, summoning back into the realm of sensation those entities long exiled beyond systems of classification—nature and the female body. This connects directly to the mechanism of ancient festivals, which temporarily suspended social order and judgment to bring forth the banished and dissolve rigid boundaries.

Within the layered silence of darkness, the gathering of information and cognitive calculation halt; the viewer moves beyond being a mere onlooker to physically absorb tremors that lie beyond language. In this moment, as explanations and social labels fall away, the walls encircling the self dissolve. Through pre-linguistic resonance, viewers break free from their isolated worlds and finally acquire a "Public Skin" that directly connects them with once-alienated existences.

Dialogue begins where naming ceases: existence not as fixed matter, but as the chaotic potentiality of randomness effaced by systems. Form of absence. High-pressure air displaces wet paint without preliminary lines, cutting trajectories of void. Born from the friction of pressure, velocity, and the body, the human figure crystallizes only in the aftermath.

The Breathing void: Upon the contingent trajectories of non-being, colors are born, live their own lives, and fade.

Because we were never a finished entity—always a fleeting condensation of countless gestures—seek not the finalized image, but the threshold of its emergence. Art disrupts the one-sided order of naming: as in a communal rite, the fear of the estranged body is not explained away, but collectively endured. Where the name falls silent, the material surface transforms into a Public Skin: a field of resonance where bodies meet one another anew. Art not as a distant assertion, but as physical, immediate touch.

Painting becomes a physical act of sensing. My body registers pressure, velocity, and temperature in real time, allowing the figure to emerge from movement rather than predetermined form.

The core of this work lies not in defining form with hand or brush, but in a physical process that deconstructs the order of pigment through the immaterial force of high-pressure air. Air pressure pushes and displaces paint across the canvas surface, generating subtle layers of density.

Here, the shape of existence emerges not as a solid, filled mass of pigment, but within the space swept away by air — in the trajectories left behind by emptiness and void. Rather than static presence, it is this trajectory of absence that becomes the most vital point of origin for existence.

When unmoored from materiality, how can floating bodies recognize and reveal the 'Skin of Knowledge' as their boundaries fade?

The exhibition opens in a dark space where visual signs stop working. Darkness isn't just there to hide the objects — it's what lets go of the thing that used to pin existence to a fixed social position and form.

In the dark, the outlines that once marked where one body ends and another begins start to blur. Losing your fixed position and floating is exactly what it takes to stop judging and start sensing.

Skin of Knowledge

The Skin of Knowledge (Haut des Wissens) names a simple fact: everything alive today gets measured, sorted, and filed by data, algorithms, efficiency. Under that layer, a person's presence gets boxed into a fixed identity, and real contact between bodies rarely happens. Thick, physical paint makes this invisible layer touchable — and asks whether it can dissolve into a Public Skin, where what lives beneath language can breathe together.

The density of the acrylic and the weight of the canvas make the Skin of Knowledge something you can feel, not just see. Thick, layered pigment marks the compressed surface of categorization — and underneath it, a living existence that hasn't gone anywhere.

I use my own body as a primordial sensor — a surface tuned to register what gets pushed outside the social order: women, nature, whatever a system files under "other."

I bring that excluded form into the room as something real — weight, paint, a body-sized presence — and let it meet the visitor's body directly, before either one of us has time to sort the other into a category.

Nothing here gets explained. It gets felt. The line each visitor uses to separate "self" from "other" loosens for a moment, and in that loosening, something opens that I call Public Skin — a shared space where people encounter one another before fixed identities take hold.

The further it moves from the material world, the thinner the canvas and pigment become. The body loses its weight. What's left, in the end, is feeling — and a blind faith that matter still matters.

Artist Statement

We perceive the world only as far as the reach of our tether permits. The artist conceptualizes the systematized knowledge layered over existence—masquerading as skin—as the "skin of knowledge," summoning onto the canvas the inherent possibilities of being hidden beneath it. Rooted in the insight that language and capital can never fully exhaust existence, this work is a critical endeavor that introduces delicate fractures into our automated everyday perception.

In this process, painting ceases to be a mere tool of representation and becomes a physical act of forming a "public skin." Pushing color with an airbrush loaded with air rather than pigment generates random trajectories where bodily breath, velocity, and atmospheric chance intersect beyond deliberate intention. The human figures emerging on the canvas are not predetermined destinations, but the temporary condensation of countless non-linear events—acting as a visceral "roar of existence."

Thus, medium is redefined not as a tool of execution, but as a physical "substrate" through which sensation and existence filter. Spanning painting, sculpture, and digital environments, the practice utilizes the material resistance of each medium to recover a sensation prior to language. Rather than dogmatically proposing a new world, the artist defamiliarizes our prescribed roles and names, establishing an aesthetic device for dialogue that opens existence not to a singular conclusion, but to "chaotic possibilities."
